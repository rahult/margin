# Margin — design context

## Design Context

### Users
Engineers, tech leads, and PMs reading documents they cannot afford to skim — RFCs, ADRs, specifications, policies, business cases. They read at a desk, in focused work sessions, often under time pressure and with real decisions riding on comprehension. The job to be done: finish a dense document and genuinely retain its argument, not just reach the bottom.

### Brand Personality
Calm, rigorous, companion-like. Three words: **quiet, sharp, humane**. The interface should evoke calm focus, earned confidence, and respect for the reader's own thinking — never hype, urgency, or manufactured reward. Margin is a reading companion, not a dashboard. Explicit anti-references: gamified productivity apps (streaks, points, badges detached from comprehension), noisy AI dashboards, "engagement" patterns that manipulate rather than serve.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial minimalism on a graphite (dark charcoal) theme. A quiet, paper-like reading surface with a muted sage-green accent on neutral charcoal grays (no pure black); serif typography for the document, a restrained sans for UI chrome. Psychology-first persuasion, not decoration: progress cues, priming questions, retrieval practice, and low-friction next steps pull the reader forward. Dark graphite theme. Motion is rare and purposeful (entrances, state changes) with smooth deceleration only.

### Design Principles
1. **The document is the hero** — chrome recedes; nothing competes with the text for attention.
2. **Momentum over metrics** — use endowed progress, the goal-gradient effect, and the Zeigarnik effect (an unfinished mission pulls you back) instead of points or streaks.
3. **Prime, then ask** — a question before each section and retrieval practice at the end; thinking prompts are low-friction and always one click away.
4. **Every element earns its place** — no dead buttons, no decorative cards, no repeated information; if it doesn't aid comprehension or momentum, it goes.
5. **Calm is a feature** — muted palette, generous whitespace, no visual noise; focus mode strips the interface to the argument itself.
